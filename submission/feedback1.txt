- Conclusion: 
    - I wouldn't structure this visibly using named paragraphs
    - Our conclusion stress more that you can optimise all three impacts at the same time (or not), as that's the novelity here
    - I think we should add to the discussion of the results that temporal shifting has less savings, but does not require to move data between regions, which sometimes will not be possible due to security/privacy concerns, and for the combination of both should give a number/range again.
    - I think threats to validity should not be presented in the conclusion but as part of a discussion at the end of the evaluation section. Each point here should become a named bullet point there. See https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.14625 for an example.
    - One threat to validity I am missing here around the spatial/temporal shifting is the assumption that there will always be resources available (though I think it is fair assumption for public clouds).
    - "... (e.g. ...) ...": that's not good writing style; let's say this without so many parentheses. I think cross-region dependability and load balancing requirements failure tolerance and low-latency delivery for customers around the world – are a good idea for future work. I also think looking at embodied carbon (and water?) makes sense and is an obvious criticism to our work. I'd drop the last point (around cost and sustainability).

- Footnotes vs. references: I'd generally use references for (peer-reviewed) paper publications and footnotes for online sources (websites, white papers, datasets, repositories – anything with mainly a link)
